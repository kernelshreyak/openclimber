# OpenClimber v2.1 Dev Log 2 — Grip Climbing, a Look Pass, and a Course to Climb

*6 October 2026*

## Where this picks up

The [first v2.1 log](v2.1-dev-log.md) ended with a cube-built climber that could attach to a `ClimbableSurface` and move across it. It worked, but it was not yet what this project is for: the body slid over the wall while the arms and legs played a fixed pose on top. This iteration replaces that with climbing that is actually carried by the hands and feet, and gives the game a proper look and a place to climb.

## The core idea: motion is computed, not played back

Nothing the character does comes from an animation clip. There is no skeleton, no `AnimationPlayer`, and no state machine of clips. Every frame, code decides where each joint should be from what the character is physically doing, and the cube segments are stretched between those joints.

- **On the ground**, a single gait phase drives both legs half a cycle apart. A foot lifts only while it is travelling forward, and the arms counter the legs. Walking and running are the same code with different stride, knee lift, lean and cadence, blended by speed.
- **In the air**, the pose comes from vertical velocity: arms reach up and one knee drives up while rising, then the arms spread and the legs reach down while falling.
- **On a wall**, the hands and feet are pinned to world-space grips and the elbows and knees are solved to fit (two-bone IK).

Switching between these never snaps: every joint eases towards its target, so ground, air, climb and pull-up blend into each other for free.

The point of doing it this way is the project's original goal. A new kind of climbable surface should not need a new animation set. If the limbs can find holds on it, the character can climb it.

## Grip climbing

This is the main change, in `scripts/climbing.gd`.

1. **Four grips.** Each hand and foot holds a fixed point on the surface, found by casting a ray from where that limb wants to be. A planted limb does not move until it is that limb's turn.
2. **One limb at a time.** Input does not move the body. It picks a direction, and the limb that is furthest behind reaches for a new hold ahead. The natural order falls out as right hand, left foot, left hand, right foot, with three points of contact kept throughout.
3. **The body is pulled, not driven.** The body is sprung towards the spot where all planted limbs would be at rest. It surges as each hold is taken and sags slightly while a limb is in the air, instead of gliding at a constant speed.
4. **The hands define the wall.** The body faces along the hands' grip normals and keeps its distance from the surface they hold, so orientation follows whatever the hands are on.
5. **Edges need no special case.** A hand can only go where there is surface to hold. At the edge of a panel the hands run out of holds and the body simply stops.
6. **Feet are optional.** Where there is nothing under a foot it comes off; with both feet off, the arms straighten and the character hangs and can shimmy on the hands alone. Feet step back on as soon as there is footing.
7. **Topping out.** When the hands cannot go higher and there is room to stand above, the character pulls up over the edge. A ledge too shallow to stand on stays a hang.

### What it is not

This is not a ragdoll with simulated muscles. The limbs are placed by rule and only the body is spring-driven. A fully physics-driven climber is hard to keep stable and controllable; this gets planted grips and weight that follows the limbs reliably, and the grips are the right foundation if the physics is pushed further later.

## Everything else that changed

**Character**
- Re-proportioned: smaller head, separate chest and waist, longer legs, tapered limbs. Height is now about 1.85 m (it was about 2.4 m), and the collision capsule covers the whole body instead of stopping at the chest.
- A consistent outfit (shirt, bare forearms, trousers, belt, climbing shoes, chalk bag) replaces the debug colours, and the face has eyes instead of a black plate.

**Movement**
- Ground movement is relative to the camera and the character turns to face where it is going.
- Heavier gravity and a stronger jump: the same height in roughly half the air time, with a faster fall than rise.
- Double jump, restored on landing or on grabbing a wall.
- Jumping off a wall turns the character away from it; `X` lets go.

**Input**
- WASD and arrow keys, plus full gamepad support (sticks, D-pad, face buttons, trigger to run). Actions are registered in `scripts/input_setup.gd`.

**World**
- Sky, lighting, fog and ambient occlusion pass; a grid shader that draws metre-accurate lines in object space, so it never stretches on resized or tilted pieces.
- Walls and panels are built from a `size` property (`scripts/box_body.gd`) instead of being scaled.
- An **obstacle course**: a first wall, a taller wall with an L-shaped route and a traverse, and a leaning slab up to a goal tower.
- A **mountain** next to it: staggered panels, a shallow hang ledge, a narrow vertical strip, a top ledge to pull over, and a summit climbed from its side face.

## Problems worth remembering

- **Feet that anchored the body.** With the hands on a ledge and the feet still planted on a strip below, the character got stuck: the body is pulled towards the average of its grips, so two feet with nowhere to go held it back. Feet now let go once they trail the body with no hold ahead.
- **The climber kept re-grabbing the wall it jumped from.** Fixed by turning away on a wall jump and by blocking re-grabs after a deliberate drop until landing.
- **Hands drifting on thin ledges.** Letting the body sag for every limb off the wall made each hand land a little lower than the last. Sag now only applies while a limb is actually reaching.
- **A character that read as squat.** Recolouring was not enough; the proportions had to change.

## Known limitations

- The limbs are rule-placed; this is grip-driven, not full physics.
- On the narrow strip both hands share the same small area and can look crowded.
- Moving between surfaces that meet at an angle (around a corner) has not been exercised.
- Overhangs and ceilings are untested.
- The mid-air jump reuses the normal rising pose.

## Tuning

All at the top of the scripts:

| What | Where | Constants |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Climb speed | `scripts/climbing.gd` | `STEP_TIME`, `STEP_LEAD` |
| How the body follows the grips | `scripts/climbing.gd` | `BODY_STIFFNESS`, `BODY_DAMPING`, `BODY_SAG` |
| Hanging | `scripts/climbing.gd` | `HANG_DROP`, `HANG_STEP_SLOWDOWN` |
| Jump and fall | `scripts/player.gd` | `JUMP_VELOCITY`, `GRAVITY_SCALE`, `FALL_GRAVITY_SCALE`, `MAX_AIR_JUMPS` |
| Pose blending | `scripts/procedural_climber.gd` | `POSE_BLEND_SPEED` |

## Validation

- Gameplay was tested by hand in the editor throughout; ground movement, jumping and controls were signed off that way.
- Scripts were syntax-checked headless with `godot --headless --check-only`.
- The grip climbing was play-tested once, which surfaced the stuck-feet problem above. The fix for it, and the speed increase that followed, still need a confirming play-through.
